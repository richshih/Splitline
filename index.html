      Splitline estimates joint positions with an on-device pose model (MoveNet) — accuracy depends on video angle, lighting and resolution, and it can lose track on baggy clothing or fast pans. The drive/stride split is inferred from trunk-lean trend, not a true event detector. Every metric's 0–10 score, its card color, and its coaching note all come from the same underlying good/poor bounds, so they can't disagree with each other — a 7+ is "good," anything lower is "watch." "Shoulder rotation", "Hip rotation" and "Braking leak" are proxies too — true side-to-side sway can't be seen from a side-on camera at all, so the rotation metrics instead read trunk/pelvis twist via shoulder and hip line width, and braking leak reads the dip in forward hip speed right after each footstrike rather than an actual ground-reaction force. Treat all of this as a directional coaching aid, not a lab-grade biomechanics report.
